Gir: The Last Kingdom of the Asiatic Lion

Deep within the dry forests of Gujarat lies Gir National Park, the sole natural habitat of the Asiatic Lion. Over 600 lions now roam these hills, thanks to conservation efforts.

Gir is a sun-drenched forest of teak and acacia, a place where the roar of the lion echoes through valleys. In 2026, it offers a chance to witness a conservation miracle and experience the rustic charm of Saurashtra.

The Maldharis: The Guardians of the Forest

History: Semi-nomadic herdsmen who have lived inside Gir for centuries, ensuring the lions' survival.

Environment: They live in circular settlements called nesses, grazing buffalo alongside lions in a pact of co-existence.

Culture: Defined by bravery and vegetarianism, their knowledge is crucial for tracking and protecting lions.

The Sasan Gir Safari: A Golden Trail

History: Former hunting reserve of the Nawab of Junagadh, hunting was banned to protect lions.

Environment: Several safari routes with dry terrain for excellent visibility, especially around waterholes in summer.

Culture: Lion trackers walk on foot through the forest using only sticks, showing the bond between humans and lions.

Somnath & Junagadh: The Sacred Gates

History: Junagadh’s Uparkot Fort has survived 15 sieges; Somnath Temple is one of the twelve Jyotirlingas.

Environment: Transition from dry forests to the Arabian Sea coastline within an hour.

Culture: Somnath provides a spiritual bookend to the wild lion safari adventure.

The Flavors of Kathiawar

Kathiawadi cuisine is spicy, rustic, and rich in garlic and green chilies.

Vagarelo Rotlo: Crumbled millet flatbread in spicy yogurt and buttermilk gravy.

Sev Tameta Nu Shaak: Tomato curry topped with crunchy chickpea noodles (Sev).

Ringna No Olo: Smoked eggplants mashed with ginger, garlic, and spring onions.

Adadiya Pak: Winter sweet made from urad dal, ghee, and warming spices.

Chaas (Buttermilk): Cold spiced buttermilk with roasted cumin and cilantro.
