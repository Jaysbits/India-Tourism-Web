Varanasi: The Eternal City of Light

Varanasi, also known as Kashi or Banaras, is a city that exists outside of time. Built along the crescent-shaped western bank of the sacred River Ganges, it has been a center of learning, civilization, and spirituality for over 3,000 years. To the devout, Varanasi is the gateway to Moksha—liberation from the cycle of rebirth. Mark Twain famously described it as "older than history, older than tradition, older even than legend, and looks twice as old as all of them put together."

The soul of Varanasi is its Ghats—the long flight of stone steps leading down to the river. Here, the entire spectrum of human existence is on display: from joyful celebrations of life and the rhythmic chanting of ancient mantras to the eternal cremation fires of Manikarnika Ghat.

In 2026, the city has undergone a major transformation with the Kashi Vishwanath Corridor, which connects the ancient temple directly to the river. Despite modernization, the city's narrow winding galis remain a maze of hidden shrines, silk weavers, incense, and woodsmoke.

🏛️ The Ghats: The Living Theater of Faith

History: There are about 84 ghats in Varanasi. Most were built or renovated during the 18th century by Maratha rulers. Each ghat carries its own legend, from the ceremonial Dashashwamedh Ghat to Assi Ghat where artists and scholars gather.

Environment: The ghats form a vertical city where buildings lean toward the river, creating a dramatic skyline of temples and palaces.

Culture: The famous Ganga Aarti takes place every evening where priests offer fire to the river using large brass lamps accompanied by chanting, bells, incense, and music.

🏘️ The Galis: The Labyrinth of Kashi

History: The inner city consists of ancient alleyways designed narrow enough to block the intense sun and heat.

Environment: These alleys are lively and crowded, often shared with vendors, sacred cows, pilgrims, and motorbikes.

Culture: This is where the legendary Banarasi Silk is woven using traditional handlooms. These sarees with gold and silver Zari threads are considered some of the finest bridal garments in India.

☸️ Sarnath: The Cradle of Buddhism

History: Located about 10 kilometers from Varanasi, Sarnath is where Buddha delivered his first sermon after attaining enlightenment.

Environment: It is a peaceful park filled with ancient stupas, monastery ruins, and archaeological structures.

Culture: The site attracts Buddhist monks and pilgrims from countries like Japan, Thailand, and Tibet, making it an international center for Buddhist learning.

The Flavors of the Holy City

Varanasi’s cuisine is largely vegetarian near the river and is known for its Satvic purity combined with vibrant street flavors.

Banarasi Paan: A famous betel leaf preparation filled with areca nut, gulkand, and spices. It is commonly eaten after meals as a palate cleanser.

Tamatar Chaat: A unique Varanasi street food made from spicy mashed tomatoes mixed with potatoes, sev, lemon, and ginger.

Kachori Sabzi: A traditional Varanasi breakfast of crispy fried kachoris served with flavorful potato curry and often accompanied by jalebi.

Malaiyo: A winter specialty made from saffron-flavored milk foam topped with pistachios and almonds.

Thandai: A cooling milk-based drink made from almonds, fennel seeds, melon seeds, and rose petals. During festivals, a version with bhang is traditionally consumed.
