The Living Root Bridges: Meghalaya’s Biological Marvels

Deep within the emerald valleys of the East Khasi Hills lie Meghalaya’s Living Root Bridges, grown over centuries by the Khasi and Jaintia tribes using Ficus elastica roots.

The Umshiang Double-Decker Bridge in Nongriat can take 15–30 years to mature and lasts over 500 years. In 2026, Meghalaya leads in "Slow Tourism," offering trekking, natural pools, and harmony between humans and nature.

The Khasi People: Guardians of the Sacred Groves

History: Matrilineal society with deep roots in Animism, where nature is inhabited by spirits.

Environment: High-altitude villages like Mawlynnong, Asia’s cleanest village.

Culture: Phawar (oral poetry) and respect for Sacred Groves, where not a single leaf may be removed.

Cherrapunji & Mawsynram: The Rain Capitals

History: Battle for the title of Wettest Place on Earth; extreme rainfall inspired the root bridges.

Environment: Tablelands with vertical drops, waterfalls like Nohkalikai Falls plunging 1,115 feet.

Culture: Locals wear Knups—bamboo umbrella hats allowing hands-free work in fields during downpours.

The Trek to Nongriat: A Journey of 3,000 Steps

History: Stone paths carved into mountainsides connected villages for centuries.

Environment: Descend 3,500 concrete and stone steps through dense jungle, humid and full of butterflies, orchids, and cicadas.

Culture: Village homestays let travelers experience the Quiet Life and meet the Root Architects guiding the trees.

The Flavors of the Khasi Hills

Khasi cuisine is earthy, using smoked meats, fermented soybeans, and local spices.

Jadh: Red rice cooked with pork or chicken, ginger, onions, and black pepper.

Doh-Khlieh: Pork salad with boiled pork, onions, and green chilies.

Tungrymbai: Fermented soybean paste sautéed with spices and pork, an acquired taste like Blue Cheese.

Sohiong: Purple fruit (black cherry) used in jams and non-alcoholic local wine.

Pukhlein: Deep-fried rice flour and jaggery snack, crispy outside, soft inside, served with hot red tea (Sha Saw).
