Kaziranga: The Stronghold of the One-Horned Giant

In the heart of the floodplains of the mighty Brahmaputra River lies Kaziranga National Park, home of the Great Indian One-Horned Rhinoceros. Thanks to conservation, it hosts over 2,400 rhinos today.

Kaziranga is a vast tapestry of elephant grass, marshy lagoons, and dense forests. It is the only place in India to spot the "Big Five": Rhino, Royal Bengal Tiger, Asiatic Elephant, Wild Water Buffalo, and Swamp Deer.

The Floodplains: A Landscape in Constant Motion

History: Survival depends on annual Brahmaputra floods that deposit nutrient-rich silt.

Environment: The park has four ranges; Central (Kohora) is popular for sightings, Eastern (Agoratoli) is a birdwatcher’s paradise.

Culture: Local Assamese communities revere the rhino; former poachers are now guides and forest guards.

The Orchid & Biodiversity Park: A Floral Masterpiece

History: One of India’s largest orchid parks.

Environment: Houses 500+ wild orchids, a bamboo garden, and a medicinal herb garden.

Culture: Witness Bihu dance performances and see traditional musical instruments like the Pepa.

Tea Estates: The Emerald Carpets

History: Assam is the world’s largest tea-growing region with estates like Hathikuli and Addabarie.

Environment: Tea gardens act as wildlife corridors between lowlands and hills, aiding animal movement during floods.

Culture: Visitors can walk through estates, watch tea-pluckers, and taste authentic Assamese tea.

The Flavors of Assam

Assamese cuisine uses minimal oil and focuses on fresh herbs and natural flavors.

Masor Tenga: Tangy fish curry with Outenga or lemon.

Khar: Starter/palate cleanser made with filtered water through banana peel ashes, cooked with raw papaya or pulses.

Aloo Pitika: Mashed potatoes with raw mustard oil, onions, green chilies, and coriander.

Bhoot Jolokia (Ghost Pepper): One of the world’s hottest chilies, used sparingly in pickles or fried fish.

Pitha: Handmade rice cakes for Bihu, sweet or savory, enjoyed with black tea.
