Gulmarg: The Meadow of Flowers and Snow

Nestled in the Pir Panjal Range of the Western Himalayas, Gulmarg is a destination of two faces. In summer, its name—meaning "Meadow of Flowers"—comes to life as the valley explodes in a carpet of daisies, lupines, and buttercups. In winter, this verdant landscape disappears under a thick, pristine blanket of powder, transforming it into one of Asia’s premier skiing destinations. Located just 56 km from Srinagar, Gulmarg has been a retreat for royalty since the 16th century and a beloved getaway for travelers seeking the crisp mountain air.

What makes Gulmarg truly world-class is the Gulmarg Gondola, one of the highest cable cars in the world, whisking visitors to a staggering 13,000 feet. From here, the views of Nanga Parbat and the surrounding Himalayan giants are unparalleled. In 2026, Gulmarg is celebrated as the heart of India's winter sports revival, offering everything from professional-grade backcountry skiing to peaceful walks through pine forests, all while maintaining the timeless charm of a Kashmiri hill station.

🏔️ The Winter Sports Arena: Apharwat Peak

History: Developed as a ski resort in 1927 by the British, it has evolved into the "High Altitude Warfare School" and a global hub for extreme sports.

Environment: The slopes on Apharwat are famous for "long-run" skiing. The powder here is often compared to the best in the Alps, but with much higher altitudes and more rugged, natural terrain.

Culture: The culture is a mix of high-adrenaline adventure and local Kashmiri resilience. You’ll see "Ski Guides" who have lived in these mountains for generations, sharing their expertise with international travelers.

⛳ The Golf Course: Greens at 8,000 Feet

History: Opened in 1911 by the British, the Gulmarg Golf Course is the highest 18-hole green course in the world.

Environment: This is not your typical flat golf course; it features undulating slopes, natural hazards, and is surrounded by dense forests of pine and fir.

Culture: Playing here is a rite of passage for golf enthusiasts. Even if you don't play, the vast meadows serve as the central social hub for evening walks and pony rides during the summer months.

⛪ Baba Reshi Shrine & St. Mary’s Church

History: Gulmarg is a testament to religious harmony. The Shrine of Baba Reshi (a 15th-century Muslim saint) and St. Mary’s Church (a Victorian-era grey stone church) have stood within miles of each other for over a century.

Environment: The shrine features beautiful Persian-style wood carvings, while the church is a classic example of British colonial architecture, often looking particularly magical when covered in snow.

Culture: The local population and tourists alike visit the Baba Reshi shrine to seek blessings for their families, reflecting the "Kashmiriyat" (the traditional ethno-national social consciousness) of the region.

🍛 The Flavors of the High Slopes

Kashmiri food in Gulmarg is designed to be deeply warming, featuring rich gravies and aromatic spices.

Roganjosh: The jewel of the Kashmiri Wazwan. Succulent pieces of lamb cooked in a vibrant red gravy made from Kashmiri chilies and flavored with ginger and fennel.

Yakhni: A delicate, yogurt-based mutton curry flavored with dried mint and cardamom. It is creamy, comforting, and less spicy than Roganjosh.

Kashmiri Kahwa: This isn't just tea; it’s an experience. A green tea infused with saffron, cinnamon, and cardamom, served with crushed almonds. It is the essential "warm-up" drink after a day on the slopes.

Dum Aloo: Baby potatoes deep-fried and then slow-cooked in a spicy, tangy gravy. The Kashmiri version is distinct for its use of mustard oil and heavy spice profile.

Noon Chai (Pink Tea): A salty, pink-colored tea brewed with bicarbonate of soda and milk. It is a traditional morning staple, usually enjoyed with a crusty local bread called Girda.
